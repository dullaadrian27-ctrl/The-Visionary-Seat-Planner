Seat Planner

If your program didn’t need you for everything... what would you build next?
Work through the three columns first, then design your 12-month role. Your answers save automatically in this browser. Download the PDF when you are done.
Before you start

Right-size this for your program

This is not a worksheet about leaving. Some owners are building toward a Visionary seat with no operations in it at all. Others want to keep teaching every morning and simply stop being the one who fixes everything. Both are real answers, and this planner is built to hold either one.

Exercise 1

Keep, transfer, create

If your program did not need you for everything... what would you build next? Start here, before you touch a calendar or an org chart.

KEEPWhat do I love doing and intentionally want to continue?
TRANSFERWhat am I currently doing that someone else should own?
CREATEWhat am I not doing because operations consume my time?
Most people rush this

They skip to TRANSFER because it feels productive, and they leave KEEP and CREATE half empty.

Those two columns are the actual work. TRANSFER is just logistics.

Teaching can go in the KEEP column

Nothing about this says you have to leave the classroom, the floor, or the children.

Plenty of owners want to stay in ratio in the morning and be the CEO in the afternoon. That is a designed role, not a failure to scale. The problem was never being in your program. The problem is your program falling apart when you are not.

Exercise 2

Design your 12-month role

Not the role you have. The role you are building toward. Write it as if it is already decided.

Zero is a legitimate answer. So is sixty.
Coverage, complaints, scheduling, putting out fires. This is the number most owners want lower.
The things that genuinely require the owner. Not the things you happen to be best at.
Be specific. “Rest” counts, but say what rest actually looks like.
Exercise 3

The bigger question

Expansion. Real estate. Another site. Another company. Consulting. Succession. Investments. Impact. Or simply more of the work that makes you feel alive.
One move. With a date on it.

Your legacy isn’t only what you leave behind. It’s also what your business allows you to live right now.

Build the business. Lead the company. Design the legacy.
